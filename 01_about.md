I am Yohan Chatelain, a scientific associate at the [Krembil Centre for Neuroinformatics](https://www.camh.ca/en/science-and-research/institutes-and-centres/krembil-centre-for-neuroinformatics), [Centre for Addiction and Mental Health (CAMH)](https://www.camh.ca), since March 2026. I am based in Montréal, Canada, and work with the [Epics lab](https://www.epics-lab.ca/).

## Research and current work

My research connects high-performance computing, scientific software, and numerical reliability. I study LLVM instrumentation, floating-point and stochastic arithmetic, mixed and reduced precision, and reproducibility in AI and neuroimaging pipelines.

I work on the [Brain Health Data Challenge](https://www.bhdc-pdsc.ca/) platform, developing computing infrastructure and evaluation methods for responsible, collaborative research with sensitive health data. My work also examines the numerical stability of neuroimaging models and longitudinal analyses.

I created [PRISM](https://github.com/verificarlo/prism) and developed [PyTracer](/software/pytracer/). I also contribute to [Verificarlo](/software/verificarlo/) and its instrumentation tools, and to [Fuzzy PyTorch](/software/fuzzy-pytorch/). My [publication list](/research) and [CV](/cv) provide the corresponding research record.

## Previous appointments and education

I was a postdoctoral researcher at CAMH from January 2025 to February 2026, and at Concordia University from September 2020 to December 2024 in [Big Data Infrastructures for Neuroinformatics](https://big-data-lab-team.github.io/index.html).

I completed a PhD in Computer Science at Université Paris-Saclay / UVSQ in 2019, a Master's in High-Performance Computing and Numerical Simulation in 2016, and a Bachelor's in Computer Science in 2014. My doctoral work at LI-PaRAD investigated numerical debugging and reduced-precision optimization of scientific applications.

## Awards and research support

My research record includes the ACM REP 2024 Best Paper Award, the Concordia Horizon Postdoctoral Fellowship (2020–2022), and an AccelNet IN-BIC research grant (2021). Details and associated work are listed in the [CV](/cv).
